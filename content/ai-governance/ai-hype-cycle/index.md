🛑 AI Hype Cycle
A Timestamped Note of Caution: Learning from the Ghosts of Tech Hype Cycles

AI feels unstoppable right now. But didn’t we say the same thing about the metaverse… blockchain… NFTs… IoT… big data… and the dot‑com boom?

Each of these technologies had real potential. Some still do.
But the hype around them often ran far ahead of architectural and economic reality.

A few years ago, virtual storefronts and corporate digital islands weren’t treated as experiments — they were treated as strategic imperatives. Budgets were approved not because the tech was ready, but because FOMO drowned out operational truth.

Today, as hundreds of billions pour into AI infrastructure, we’re watching the same script replay itself.

To be clear: today’s machine learning systems are powerful and transformative in the right contexts. But innovation only matters if the architecture beneath it can sustain long‑term enterprise weight.

Before the market forces a correction, it’s worth pausing and asking three unglamorous, first‑principles questions:

1. Are we building a proprietary product — or funding a commodity?
Open‑source models are accelerating, and token costs are trending toward zero.
If the intelligence layer becomes a cheap utility, where does the enterprise moat live?
Are we spending premium capital to build custom wrappers around a commodity?

2. How do we bridge the “Last Mile” deficit?
In enterprise systems, 80% accuracy is a failure mode.
If a model introduces silent hallucinations, it forces a human‑in‑the‑loop to audit 100% of the output.
Once we account for governance, validation, and risk controls, does the automation math still hold — or are we simply shifting labor costs?

3. Have we mapped the physical limits of our infrastructure?
AI runs on hardware that consumes unprecedented electrical power.
Data center demand is scaling exponentially, while national grids face multi‑year bottlenecks.
What happens when the utility grid becomes the real constraint on AI scale?

The Takeaway
History shows that when tech cycles are driven by corporate anxiety rather than architectural need, capital gets deployed efficiently into infrastructure — but inefficiently into solving real problems.

The technologies that endure aren’t the ones that impress in demos.
They’re the ones that quietly deliver predictable, high‑margin ROI to the core business.

I’m timestamping this now, while optimism is at its peak, because returning to first principles is easier before the correction arrives.

A Final Reflection
Billions were invested — and lost — in past hype cycles. Some companies didn’t just write off projects; they went under because they followed FOMO instead of architectural reality.

What is to say we’re not repeating the same mistakes?
If I had to estimate, fewer than 5% of companies today have true, high‑value AI use cases. The rest are moving because everyone else is moving.

That doesn’t make AI bad.
It just means the behavior around AI looks very familiar.

For the architects, delivery leads, and strategists:
How is your organization balancing the pressure to innovate with the realities of long‑term architectural stability?
