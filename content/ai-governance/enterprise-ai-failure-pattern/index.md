Next biggest opportunity isn’t AI
It’s surviving the tech‑debt bomb AI is about to create.

General George S. Patton said, “If everyone is thinking alike, then somebody isn’t thinking.”

Right now, every company is stampeding into AI out of pure FOMO.
But here’s the part no one wants to say out loud:

AI will fail in 95%+ of enterprises — not because AI is immature, but because the enterprise is.

Most companies don’t even have a unified data, digital, or application platform.
They don’t have harmonized semantics.
They don’t have curated, governed, production‑ready data.
They don’t have lineage, quality, or cross‑system consistency.
They don’t have real AI use cases — just wishful thinking and vendor decks.

So what happens?
They bolt AI onto fragmented systems, build adaptors, stitching layers, shadow pipelines, and “temporary” integrations that become permanent.
Every shortcut compounds.
Every workaround multiplies.
Every model adds another dependency.

This isn’t tech debt you can clean up later.
This is irreversible tech debt — the kind that spreads across systems, vendors, workflows, and compliance boundaries until the cost of fixing it exceeds the value of the company itself.

A few narrow AI wins will exist.
But enterprise‑wide AI?
That’s where the collapse happens.

The real risk isn’t “missing the AI wave.”
It’s drowning in the aftermath.

Everyone is thinking alike.
Somebody needs to start thinking.

I’m sharing this now as a marker in time. The signs are visible, but most are overlooking them — or not seeing them at all.
Years from now, this will read like an early alarm that should have been taken seriously.
